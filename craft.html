01

The fibre

True pashmina begins 14,000 feet up, in the undercoat of the Changthangi goat. Each animal yields barely 80 grams a year — combed, never sheared, every spring.

14–16µfibre fineness
02

The loom

Hand-spun on the yinder wheel and woven on wooden looms unchanged for generations. A kaani shawl grows a few centimetres a day, following a coded pattern called the talim.

18 mofor one kaani shawl
03

The hand

Walnut carved with hand chisels, papier-mâché painted with squirrel-hair brushes, carpets knotted one thread at a time. No two pieces are ever the same — by intention.

900+knots per sq. inch

Dal Lake, Srinagar — where every piece begins

“Every shawl remembers the hands that made it.”

— Ghulam Nabi, master weaver, fourth generation · Kanihama village

We work directly with 42 artisan families across the valley. Every piece carries the name of its maker, and every purchase returns to the workshop that made it.
